# Early Game Review

## Reading the Rule book

> When judging robots, one of the things that catches a few judge's eyes is the lack of any piece of the rule book or info that would've been received by reading the rule book. How would you know what dimensions to make your robot without reading the rule book? Reading the rule book should always be the beginning of your notebook since its will always be the basis of your robot/engineering design cycle.

## What do you need from the rule book?

> For the development of the robot, the rule book gives you the dimensions of the field, game pieces, robot dimensions (usually the same), and robot extension limits. And for your matches, the rules are _very_ important. Going to matches without knowing how to play is going to move you directly to the red flag / no pick list in an instant.

## What do you need to have in your notebook?

> Your notebook shouldn't be a list of every rule, but only the pieces of information that may effect your match strategy and the information that may change the design of the robot. These pieces of information can be noted in the section you use for rule book review and should be reused in the brainstorming section of the design process.
